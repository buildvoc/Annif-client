- Claim/snippet: Thomas had an appraising eye for public houses, noting many in Farnham which, being in hop-growing country, was well supplied with them. Originally, they would have served yeoman farmers and labourers as well as the many pilgrims heading for Canterbury along the Pilgrim's Way. Hops and beer-making later became a staple of Farnham's economy: John Aubrey said that hop-growing began in the area in the late 16th century. It flourished, particularly from the 18th century, because of the encouraging combination of the slopes on either side of the River Wey, a supportive temperate climate and a moderate rainfall. By 1800 there were with over 1000 acres of hop grounds around the town and there were regional hop grounds in nearby Alton and as far south as Petersfield. Farnham's hops, particularly its 'Whitebine', were highly prized. The building of nearby Aldershot as an Army camp in 1854 led to an influx of military personnel who enjoyed Farnham's services; many pubs were built to meet the demand. Local brewing companies amalgamated in 1889 as Farnham United Breweries. Peak acreage of 1,592 acres had been achieved in 1875 and the early Autumn hop-picking and drying season dominated the town. Local writer George Sturt quotes Grover, his gardener, as saying of 19 th century Farnham, 'A good many men used to make purty near a livin' goin' round brewin' for people.'
